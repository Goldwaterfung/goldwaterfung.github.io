Context and Floor Observation

MRKE operated as a physical fast-turnaround haircut business serving a community of 10 residential blocks. As co-founder, I formulated service pricing, evaluated unit economics, and established our speed-to-quality benchmarks. Our business model depended on delivering reliable haircut quality within short customer turnaround windows.

Extensive on-site floor observation revealed a continuous operational bottleneck: flat 2D catalog photos caused severe visual ambiguity. Customers struggled to picture how a flat haircut photo would translate to their personal head shape, layering, and neckline tapers.

Initial consultations dragged to 4 to 6 minutes of hesitant back-and-forth, creating customer anxiety and breaking our fast-turnaround targets.

Through direct floor observation, I also discovered that the shop served a wide cross-generational customer base ranging from 6-year-old children to 70-year-old elderly residents. Children aged 6 to 12 and teenagers were eager digital adopters who lacked specialized styling terminology to communicate layering and volume, while senior customers up to age 70 needed high-contrast visuals without complex menus or small text. This insight proved that consultation friction was an interaction and communication problem across age groups, requiring a minimal-text visual interface that any customer could operate immediately without reading barriers.

Before deploying the 360 hairstyle viewer application to the shop floor, I conducted early senior usability testing by giving the prototype to my grandmother to use first. Observing where she hesitated or mis-tapped allowed me to iterate the button designs and simplify navigation controls for older adults. I then deployed the application to the store rapidly and used store CCTV footage alongside live floor observation to watch unprompted customer behavior while interacting with the tablet, continuously iterating the layout based on real behavioral patterns.

Design Exploration and Discarded Iterations

To resolve consultation ambiguity without slowing down store operations, I explored multiple concepts before arriving at the final software architecture:

Granular Parametric Sliders

Detailed length, volume, and fade sliders caused analysis paralysis for customers, frustrated stylists under a 30-second rush-hour target, and doubled consultation duration.

Cloud-Rendered WebGL

Budget station Android tablets faced strict GPU thermal limits and periodic network latency. Streaming models via WebGL risked frame drops and store service interruption.

Printed Lookbooks

Static multi-angle printouts could not provide continuous 360-degree rotation, and updating seasonal haircuts incurred high recurring printing costs.

Lightweight 360 Preset Viewer

Built a responsive Unity C# engine running locally on Android tablets with instant touch rotation, curated preset categories, and full-screen standing stylist touch ergonomics.

UI Design and In-Store Deployment

To reduce cognitive overload for customers ranging from age 6 to 70, I structured the screen-to-screen interaction flow around minimal taps and minimal on-screen text:

1 1 Tap, Minimal Text

Visual Category Entry

High-contrast visual category thumbnails allow children and elderly customers up to age 70 to pick a hairstyle group immediately without reading dense text labels.

2 1 Tap Selection

Curated 3D Preset Load

Tapping a portrait card loads the corresponding 3D head model directly into the viewport, eliminating multi-step configuration menus or granular sliders.

3 Direct Touch Gesture

Full-Screen 360 Touch Orbit

The entire screen acts as a forgiving touch surface where users swipe horizontally to rotate 360 degrees or tilt vertically to inspect crown volume and neckline tapers.

4 Zero Typing

Shared Visual Confirmation

Seated customers and standing stylists align on the exact 3D angle together in under two minutes, transitioning straight into the haircut.

I engineered the complete software lifecycle to integrate seamlessly with shop floor routines:

  • User Journey Mapping and Minimal-Text UI: Mapped consultation steps from greeting and chair handover to style selection, 360-degree rotation, and final cutting confirmation, keeping text to an absolute minimum so every age group from 6 to 70 could navigate intuitively.
  • Prototype Testing and Button Iteration: Tested early versions of the 360 hairstyle viewer application with my grandmother before store launch, identifying touch hesitation points and iterating button sizes and placement for senior accessibility.
  • Unobtrusive CCTV Behavioral Observation and Stylist Logs: Deployed rapidly to shop tablets and combined daily stylist tracking logs with store CCTV behavioral observation to watch how customers naturally held and tapped the screen. Observing stylists operating with damp hands and customers reaching for the screen led me to expand touch targets to full-screen swipe rotation and added vertical camera pitch tilt to inspect crown volume and neckline tapers.
  • Dedicated Youth Presets: Based on daily tracking data confirming high engagement among youth customers, I prioritized a dedicated library of 3D youth hairstyles, enabling young customers to point and rotate their preferred style with confidence.
  • Google Cloud Storage Over-the-Air Asset Streaming: Architected automated deployment pipelines with Google Cloud Storage for dynamic over-the-air asset streaming. Stylists received new 3D model updates without taking tablets out of service or disrupting daily store operations.

Technical Stack

The system was architected for fast on-site execution with zero maintenance downtime during salon operations:

  • Interactive 3D Client Runtime (Unity C#): Custom touch viewer built with optimized camera orbit controls, touch drag gestures, vertical pitch tilt, and preset model loading designed for low-power mobile GPUs.
  • Target Hardware (Android OS): Deployed to dedicated Android tablets mounted directly at haircutting stations, configured with locked kiosk parameters to prevent thermal throttling and accidental app exits during work hours.
  • Asset Delivery Pipeline (Unity Addressables with GCS Streaming): Configured Unity Addressables to stream compressed 3D hairstyle asset bundles over the air from Google Cloud Storage buckets. New seasonal haircuts and styling collections loaded dynamically in the background without requiring application rebuilds, manual side-loading, or device downtime.
  • Interaction and Journey Prototyping (Figma): Designed touch interaction flows, large touch target hitboxes for standing stylists with damp hands, and streamlined navigation paths for quick style confirmation.

Practical Outcomes and Business Impact

Measurable Operational Result

Customers and stylists rotated 3D models 360 degrees before cutting, achieving confident alignment in under two minutes. Consultation ambiguity and discussion time fell approximately 35 percent across hundreds of live sessions.

The 360 hairstyle viewer application transformed indecisive consultations into confident agreements within seconds. Floor observation proved that the most enthusiastic users were youth customers, validating the decision to prioritize dedicated youth styles. The project confirmed that software built to solve frontline operational bottlenecks creates immediate commercial utility.

Lessons Learned and Retrospective

Software built for fast physical operations must prioritize immediate ergonomics over feature density. Early concepts explored rich customization controls, but stylists needed style confirmation within twenty seconds during rush hours. The simplest interface adopted consistently on the shop floor delivers far greater commercial value than feature-heavy software that interrupts the service rhythm.
