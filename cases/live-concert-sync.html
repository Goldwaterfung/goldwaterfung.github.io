Context and Live Stage Problem

For a premier live Erhu performance in a 1,000-seat university concert hall, our engineering team was tasked with projecting an interactive 3D virtual avatar driven live by the master musician's bow gestures using vision-based AI pose tracking.

During early rehearsals, rapid bowing passages broke raw vision tracking. Inference frame drops and optical motion blur produced severe visual jitter. The avatar repeatedly snapped between erratic limb positions, drifting out of sync with the live acoustic audio in the hall.

Design Exploration and Discarded Iterations

Before establishing our buffer architecture, we tested multiple technical approaches to resolve tracking instability on stage:

Unbuffered Zero-Latency Stream

Direct streaming from raw pose inference to Unity animation rigs caused aggressive visual snapping whenever optical dropouts occurred under hall lighting.

Mathematical Smoothing Filters

Applying heavy Kalman filters introduced continuous phase lag during rapid bowing direction changes, making the avatar appear sluggish and out of sync with acoustic note attacks.

Cadence-Calibrated Buffer

Shadowed rehearsals to capture the musician's physical preparation, designing a 1.5-second deterministic streaming buffer that absorbed tracking variance while locking visual bowing to acoustic audio.

Engineering Interventions and Rehearsal Calibration

Rather than trying to force zero-latency tracking that caused jitter, I calibrated system buffers to the natural physical cadence of the live instrument:

  • Musician Rehearsal Shadowing: Attended rehearsals alongside the musician to analyze musical cadences, discovering that the performer executed an organic ~0.8-second preparatory physical motion before fast bowing phrases.
  • Deterministic 1.5-Second Streaming Buffer: Implemented a 1.5-second streaming buffer between vision pose inference and avatar animation playback in Unity. This buffer absorbed inference variance and temporal micro-jitters without breaking visual stability.
  • Waveform and Keypoint Alignment: Recorded motion data alongside audio waveforms to verify that avatar bowing stroke direction changes aligned with acoustic note attacks.
  • Full-Dress Stage Simulations: Validated the pipeline across repeated full-dress hall simulations under concert lighting and stage acoustics.

Practical Outcomes and Performance Impact

Live Performance Reliability

The virtual avatar stayed locked to the live Erhu performance throughout the concert with zero visual snapping. Acoustic clarity and visual cadence remained cohesive before 1,000 live audience members.

The performance demonstrated that solving live real-time synchronization requires understanding the physical, domain-specific cadence of the human performer, rather than relying on unbuffered algorithms.

Lessons Learned and Retrospective

Engineering real-time synchronization on a live stage cannot be solved purely through lower algorithmic latency. When we attempted unbuffered tracking, frame drops created unacceptable visual snapping.

Respecting the performer's organic physical preparation and introducing a calibrated 1.5-second buffer grounded in acoustic note attacks produced a rock-solid stage performance that raw algorithms could not achieve alone.
