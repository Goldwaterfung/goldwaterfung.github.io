Stratawright — Next-Generation Digital Audio Workstation Natively Supporting AI Agent CLINEW

Translating digital audio signals into structured text readable by LLM agents, enabling AI agents to control multitrack audio engineering precisely via command line.

Why did I initiate this project? Traditional DAW software was designed for keyboard and mouse workflows. When projects expand beyond 100 tracks, over 50% of creator cognitive load is consumed by repetitive mechanical operations such as memorizing shortcuts, routing tracks, and balancing volumes. Existing DAWs lack signal and control interfaces directly accessible by LLMs, hindering AI agents from entering audio production pipelines.
What problem does this project solve? Constructing an audio workstation architecture natively supporting AI Agent CLI. The core innovation translates low-level audio signals (peak dB, LUFS loudness, spectral energy distribution, and waveform characteristics) into structured text and JSON formats parseable by agents. Simultaneously, it provides a bidirectional CLI protocol enabling agents such as Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, or Gemini to execute audio analysis, track grouping, gain staging, panning, and DSP plugin automation commands, establishing an efficient text-signal feedback loop to organize and pre-mix 100+ track projects within seconds.

Measuring Visual Capture in Virtual Reality under Sustained Audiovisual Conflict with Third-Order Ambisonics

Exploring sensory boundaries under audiovisual conflict in virtual reality, establishing cross-modal design principles for future smart glasses.

Why did I conduct this research? As smart glasses and spatial computing enter mainstream adoption, digital information blends deeply with physical space. This research investigates the boundaries of human sensory perception during cross-modal conflict: when auditory and visual cues contradict, which sense does the human brain prioritize?
Fung, K. K., Saw, T. W., & Rajapakse, R. P. C. J. (2026). Accepted for publication at IEEE ICVR 2026.

Three Key Experimental and Academic Contributions:

  1. Experimental Paradigm Innovation: Pioneered a decoupled head-gaze and hand-pointing tracking paradigm to quantify true sensory preferences and dynamic correction trajectories under audiovisual interference.
  2. Empirical Evidence of Sensory Capture and Cognitive Cost: Demonstrated that even with 16-channel Third-Order Ambisonics high-fidelity spatial audio, salient visual distractors overwhelmingly dominate initial localization, with cross-modal conflict incurring significant cognitive costs (reaction times prolonged by 1.6 seconds).
  3. Foundations for Spatial Computing Perception: Grounded in cognitive psychology, proved that spatial audio alone cannot counteract visual misalignment, establishing theoretical foundations and design guidelines for cross-modal audiovisual coherence in smart glasses and spatial computing.

1. Physical-Digital Retail Integration — 3D Hairstyle Interactive Showcase and Waiting Area Experience App

Role: Product Manager & Co-Founder Timeline: 2023 - 2026 Company: MRKE Ltd.
Deployed dedicated in-store tablets supporting hundreds of consultations
Reduced consultation communication time by ~35%
On-site behavioral observation drove roadmap pivot

Business Background and Problem Discovery

In physical retail operations, customers spent an average of 15 to 20 minutes waiting with nothing to do, leading to restlessness and impatience. Furthermore, during consultations, a significant information gap existed between 2D reference photos and actual 3D haircuts, resulting in excessive stylist communication costs and fluctuating customer satisfaction.

Product Goals and Scope

As Product Manager and Co-Founder, the core objective I established for the team was: deploy dedicated Android tablets in salon waiting areas and integrate them into stylists' daily consultation SOPs without increasing store labor costs, reducing hairstyle selection consultation time by at least 30% while transforming tedious wait times into an engaging interactive discovery experience.

Team Collaboration, Research, and Data-Driven Roadmap Pivot

  • Interview and Survey Design: I designed a 5-question post-visit survey and interviewed over 50 customers alongside salon staff. Findings revealed that 72% of customers experienced primary anxiety from the inability to preview 360-degree hairstyle details.
  • Cross-Role On-Site Behavioral Observation: Following the MVP launch, I enlisted stylists to record app usage patterns during daily handovers. Data and observations revealed that children and teenagers accompanying their parents engaged most actively and had the longest session times.
  • Dynamic Roadmap Adjustment and Team Consensus: I presented these insights during weekly team meetings and collaborated with stylists to select 5 popular styles preferred by parents for initial 3D modeling, quickly prioritizing and deploying children's 3D models to enhance family customer experiences.
  • Technical and SOP Integration: Architected client-cloud pipeline connecting Unity to Google Cloud Storage (GCS) for over-the-air 3D asset streaming; established polygon count and texture compression specifications to ensure 60fps performance on mobile hardware; trained stylists to incorporate the app into daily haircut consultation SOPs, bridging the gap between digital preview and physical execution.
  1. Waiting Experience Perception: Approximately how long did you wait for consultation today? Would interactive content enhance your waiting experience?
  2. Consultation Information Gap: When referencing 2D hairstyle photos, is it difficult to visualize the dynamic 360-degree silhouette and overall outcome after the haircut?
  3. Style Selection Pain Points: What is your primary concern when communicating with the stylist? (e.g., concerns about side/crown volume not matching expectations, uncertainty about fit with personal face shape)
  4. 3D Interaction Intent: Would a tablet enabling 360-degree rotation and zoom for 3D hairstyle previews help you articulate your preferences more accurately to your stylist?
  5. Value and Satisfaction: Does converting waiting time into self-guided exploration via a 3D app improve your overall satisfaction with the salon service?

Product Impact and Business Outcomes

Following the deployment of dedicated Android tablets in salon waiting areas, the app supported hundreds of live customer consultations and became fully integrated into stylists' daily consultation SOPs. Customer hairstyle selection time decreased by approximately 35%, significantly reducing information asymmetry, while turning idle waiting into interactive exploration, boosting family return visits and overall salon satisfaction.

Strategic Reflection and Key Takeaways

An MVP launch is not the finish line, but the starting point for learning. Relying solely on initial quantitative surveys would have missed the critical children's segment opportunity; only by staying on the front lines and leveraging daily team observations can genuine product opportunities be captured with agility.

2. B2B Medical 3D Simulation and LLM Clinical Diagnosis Training System — Internship UX Diagnostics and Optimization Recommendations

Role: UX Product Intern Organization: Innova Medical Technology | Kaohsiung, Taiwan Timeline: Jul - Aug 2024
Achieved complete alignment between 3D symptoms and database records
Analyzed 50 clinical cases to pinpoint core experience bottlenecks

Business Background and Problem Identification

Innova Medical developed a B2B clinical diagnostic training system combining LLM dialogue with 3D virtual patients for medical students conducting physical examinations and patient interviews. Initial testing revealed significant diagnostic misjudgments during physical examination modules, undermining training efficacy.

Internship Responsibilities and Discovery Scope

As a UX Product Intern, my primary mission was to: assist the team during the internship period in diagnosing the root causes behind student misjudgments and deliver actionable recommendations with prioritization frameworks.

Hands-On Product Testing, Issue Identification, and Symptom Alignment

  • Observing and Diagnosing Root Causes: Initial team assumptions presumed that unpolished 3D art assets caused student distraction. Through hands-on product walkthroughs and comprehensive data comparisons, I helped the team identify two fundamental root causes: an audiovisual desynchronization between virtual patient animations and LLM voice input, and the absence of subtle visual symptoms described in the database across more than half of the 50 virtual cases.
  • Product Testing and Database Cross-Validation: Through repeated hands-on testing cross-referenced against database records, I confirmed that misdiagnoses concentrated around audiovisual latency and missing textual symptom visual cues, which prevented accurate visual inspection.
  • Visual Symptom Optimization and Presentation: I directly refined 3D virtual patient visual assets to achieve full alignment with database symptom records. Concurrently, I synthesized diagnostic findings into a team presentation, guiding engineering resources to reprioritize from cosmetic art upgrades toward rebuilding audiovisual synchronization and implementing missing clinical visual details.

Testing Validation and Experience Outcomes

Following synchronization refactoring and visual feature refinements, the 3D virtual patient visual presentations achieved full parity with database clinical descriptions. This resolved diagnostic confusion stemming from audiovisual latency and missing visual symptoms, validating a root-cause optimization methodology grounded in direct product testing and database validation.

Internship Reflection and Key Takeaways

The first step in problem-solving is not accepting existing assumptions, but directly experiencing the product and validating against underlying data. The most valuable lesson was learning to support team decisions through objective user experience testing and database cross-referencing, establishing development priorities far more effectively than subjective aesthetic debates.

3. AR and RAG-Integrated Multimodal AI Real-Time Virtual Principal

Role: Project Assistant / Systems Integration (National Tainan University of the Arts, Office of Academic Affairs Project) Timeline: Oct 2024 – Mar 2025
Zero mid-dialogue drop-offs across dozens of live showcase sessions
Unity state machine visual buffering bridged 3-5s inference latency
Taipei AI team multimodal pipeline & university deployment

Project Background and Objectives

Initiated by the Dean of Academic Affairs, this campus innovation project aimed to develop and deploy an AR virtual principal capable of real-time voice dialogue in physical environments. During showcase and reception testing, on-device multimodal inference across the Taipei AI team's pipeline (Whisper ASR, Llama 3 with RAGFlow, and Fish-Speech TTS) on a single 24GB VRAM local GPU required 3 to 5 seconds. This silent waiting period frequently led visitors to assume system stalls and abandon interactions. My responsibility was to integrate the Taipei AI team's multimodal AI system into Unity, implement an animation state machine transition (such as attentive nodding and reflective chin-touching) to buffer user-perceived delay under fixed backend compute latency, and deploy the virtual principal live at the university.

Cross-Team Collaboration and Solutions

  • Designing Unity Animation State Machine Transitions: To bridge the 3-to-5-second inference latency, I implemented an animation state machine transition in Unity. While the backend AI processes responses, the virtual principal immediately triggers real-time attentive nodding or reflective chin-touching animations, signaling to visitors that the system is actively listening and processing.
  • Defining Emotion-Driven Facial Linkages: Collaborated with the Taipei-based AI team to embed sentiment tags in response payloads. Upon receiving tags, the frontend immediately drives corresponding emotional expressions and lip-sync blendshapes, substantially enhancing conversational realism.
  • Incorporating Spatial Awareness: Integrated spatial cameras for head tracking. When visitors moved around the avatar, its gaze and head orientation smoothly tracked visitor movement.
  • Aligning Cross-Team Communication Timings: With frontend visuals and backend AI developed by distributed teams, I proactively aligned packet sequencing protocols (audio and text payload timings), ensuring precise audio-animation synchronization and preventing lip-sync lag.

Project Outcomes and Experience Enhancement

Publicly showcased and deployed at university innovation exhibitions and VIP receptions, the system handled dozens of multi-turn real-time voice dialogues. Without incurring additional hardware or compute overhead, Unity animation state machine visual buffering and communication timing alignment bridged perceived inference delay, achieving a 0% visitor drop-off rate and delivering a seamless spatial AR virtual principal experience.

Reflection and Key Takeaways

Transitioning from executing specifications to proactively resolving experience friction. This experience demonstrated that engineering extends beyond passive requirement execution; understanding user-side friction enables proactive solutions within one's technical scope (such as state machine design), fostering effective cross-team collaboration.

4. Chinese Traditional Erhu Live — AI Motion Capture and Real-Time Audiovisual Synchronization System

Role: Assistant Technical Integration Engineer (National Tainan University of the Arts, Office of Academic Affairs Project) Timeline: Sep 2023 – Jun 2024
Public live performance in university concert hall
Cross-domain translation between artistic experience and technical metrics
1.5s fixed buffer achieved stage-grade audiovisual alignment

Background and Challenges

Integrating virtual avatar systems for live traditional Chinese Erhu performances. Highly dynamic bowing movements and continuous audio streams posed severe audiovisual desynchronization and compute latency challenges during live performances before hundreds of audience members in a major university concert hall.

Cross-Domain Metric Translation and Scope Definition

As Assistant Technical Engineer, my primary objective was to: identify the optimal synchronization threshold between bowing visuals and audio data streams without compromising high-fidelity sound quality, achieving stage-grade performance stability.

Language Translation and Engineering Trade-Offs

  • Bridging Art and Engineering Terminology: While engineers focused on millisecond latency, performers prioritized visual rhythm and the organic attack of bowing motions. I worked directly in rehearsal spaces with the Erhu performer, converting artistic breathing and phrasing into quantifiable frame and buffer metrics for the engineering team.
  • Collaborative Testing and Engineering Compromise: Recording motion capture data and audio waveforms during rehearsals, I identified an ~0.8s pre-attack bowing window. Collaborating with audio engineers, we implemented a 1.5s fixed stable buffer, aligning motion capture tracking with high-fidelity audio streams.
  • On-Site Rehearsals and Confidence Building: Conducted multiple live stage simulations with the performer to verify that virtual avatar responsiveness matched playing tactile feedback, ensuring performance confidence.

Stage Performance and Quantitative Outcomes

Balancing engineering trade-offs while preserving pristine audio fidelity, implementing a 1.5s fixed buffer achieved frame-accurate synchronization between audio streams and AI motion capture data, culminating in a successful live concert hall performance before hundreds of attendees.

Strategic Reflection and Key Takeaways

The core of cross-domain collaboration lies in redefining problems through the counterpart's language. When technical metrics conflict with artistic intent, actively establishing shared vocabulary and balanced compromises ensures successful project delivery.
